# Design review

Source: *Magnifica Humanitas*, mainly §14, §46–85, §95–111, §156, §164.

These are questions for the humans who decide. Most of the encyclical binds whoever designs, finances, regulates, and deploys [§9, §111], not the artifact. The agent's job is to ask, record the answers, and point out where an answer is missing.

## How to use

1. Answer in writing. "Not applicable" needs a reason.
2. An unanswered question in A, C, or G blocks the decision until a named person owns it.
3. Keep the answers with the design record. They are the accountability trail [§105].

## A. Dignity

- Who is classified, scored, or selected? What does the system measure, ignore, and optimize? [§104]
- Does any output treat some people as less worthy, less useful, or expendable? [§51, §117]
- Can an affected person appeal? To whom, how fast, with what power to reverse? [§104, §164]
- Does the design leave room for a person to change? [§102, §128]

## B. Common good and universal destination of goods

- Who benefits, who bears the costs? Are costs shifted onto people who get none of the benefit? [§83, §84]
- Does this concentrate data, compute, or decision power, or widen access? [§67, §95, §108]
- Whose data is this? Did contributors have a say? Could it be governed as a shared good? [§108, §178]
- Is access paired with the education needed to use it? [§109]

## C. Subsidiarity and accountability

- At what level is each decision made? Could it sit closer to the people affected? [§70]
- Can affected communities choose and correct, or only observe after standards were set elsewhere? [§109]
- Who designs, trains, authorizes, operates? Is each link named and answerable? [§105, §199]
- Is there independent oversight, or only self-assessment? [§71, §106]
- Is the ethical framework inside the system open to discussion by those it affects? [§107]

## D. Solidarity

- Who does the hidden work: labeling, moderation, training, extraction? Under what conditions? [§109, §173]
- Has the supply chain had due diligence for forced labor and exploitation? [§179]
- What is the impact on people far away and on those who come later? [§76]

## E. Social justice

- Start from the most vulnerable affected person. What happens to them? [§78]
- Does the system reproduce discrimination through opaque logic? How was that tested? [§80]
- Was justice a design input from the outset, or a patch for later? [§109, §161]

## F. Work

- Verifiable measures for employment, retraining, and worker participation? [§156]
- Is the dignity of work among the success indicators? [§156]
- Does the tool free human time, or force people to the machine's pace? [§150]

## G. Truth and freedom

- Can this generate or amplify content that blurs fact and falsehood? [§132]
- Does the business model depend on capturing attention or exploiting weakness? [§170]
- Does it profile, predict, or nudge without people knowing? [§171]
- Are minors exposed? Which protections sit with the provider? [§141, §142]

## H. Environment

- Energy, water, hardware footprint. Is a smaller model, or no model, adequate? [§101]

## I. Pace

- Is "inevitable" being used as an argument? [§106]
- Is adoption outrunning the safeguards that would govern it? What would slowing down cost other than advantage? [§106]
- Is this driven by a race for dominance, commercial or geopolitical? [§110]

## J. The closing test

- Gate question: does this make human life more human in every aspect, for everyone it touches and for future generations? [§85, §129]
- Babel or Nehemiah: one direction imposed from above for efficiency, or each party responsible for a section of the wall, with relationships rebuilt before structures? [§7–10, §13]
- Practices the text names: responsible planning, human and social impact assessment, inclusion of the most vulnerable, digital literacy [§14]. Which has this decision actually had?
