# Three articles on the Elm Street bike lanes (fictional town of Riverton)

## 1. Riverton Gazette, "Elm Street lanes open after two-year fight"

The protected bike lanes on Elm Street opened Saturday. The city removed 84 parking spaces to build them. Council member Ortiz said the lanes "connect the east side to downtown for people who don't drive." A group of Elm Street shop owners has filed a petition with 312 signatures asking for the parking to be restored. The city's traffic office reports that morning car travel times on Elm rose by about two minutes in the first week.

## 2. Riverton Public Radio, "Bike counts up, shop owners split"

Automated counters recorded 1,140 bike trips on Elm Street in the first full week, against 260 in the same week last year. Of nine shop owners interviewed, four said foot traffic was down, three said it was unchanged, two said it was up. The owner of Elm Hardware said deliveries are harder without curb parking. The owner of the corner café said weekend sales were the best in a year.

## 3. Riverton Gazette, opinion, "Nobody asked us"

Letter from a resident of the 400 block: the city held two public meetings, both on weekday mornings, and the lanes were decided before the second one. The writer supports bike infrastructure in principle but says the process ignored people who work days. The letter asks for a third meeting in the evening and for the parking on the 400 block to be reviewed.
